# RLU Stage 5 — A Minimal Route Beyond General Relativity

## Status statement

This note constructs a controlled infrared extension of RLU-C that strictly contains general relativity and makes two classes of non-GR predictions. It proves several conditional mathematical statements and one exact black-hole scalar profile. It does **not** establish that the new coefficients are nonzero in nature, that the microscopic graph flows to this action, or that the theory is empirically superior to GR.

The central result is that RLU has exactly two defensible routes beyond Einstein gravity in four dimensions:

1. allow the gauge-invariant norm of the Cartan order parameter to remain dynamical;
2. retain higher-curvature operators generated by coarse-graining the relational graph, interpreted perturbatively as an effective field theory.

A local four-dimensional theory built only from the metric and yielding second-order equations otherwise collapses to GR plus a cosmological constant under the assumptions of Lovelock's theorem.

## 1. Minimal parity-even infrared action

Let the Cartan vector be written in the broken phase as

\[
V^A=(\ell+\sigma)n^A,
\qquad n^An_A=1,
\]

and let \(\varphi\) denote the canonically normalized, dimensionless low-energy radial mode. The minimal parity-even RLU extension is

\[
\boxed{
S_{\mathrm{RLU}+}
=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int d^4x\sqrt{-g}
\left[
R-\frac12(\nabla\varphi)^2
+\alpha\,\varphi\,\mathcal G
+c_e\ell_*^4\,\mathcal O_e
+O(\partial^8)
\right]
+S_m[g,\Psi].
}
\]

Here

\[
\mathcal G
=R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
-4R_{\mu\nu}R^{\mu\nu}
+R^2
\]

is the four-dimensional Euler/Gauss-Bonnet density, and

\[
\mathcal O_e
=C_{\mu\nu}{}^{\rho\sigma}
C_{\rho\sigma}{}^{\alpha\beta}
C_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\mu\nu}
\]

is the parity-even cubic Weyl invariant. The dimensions are

\[
[\alpha]=L^2,
\qquad
[\ell_*]=L,
\qquad
[c_e]=1.
\]

The parity-odd operators

\[
\varphi\,R\widetilde R,
\qquad
C C\widetilde C
\]

may be added later. If the microscopic graph action is invariant under orientation reversal, both are forbidden at the matching scale because they are pseudoscalars. The parity-even action above is therefore the sharpest initial target.

## 2. Why these terms arise from RLU-C

The general polynomial Cartan action built from \(V^A\), its covariant derivative, and the \(SO(1,4)\) curvature contains an Euler-type term

\[
\alpha_1(V^2)\,\epsilon_{ABCDE}V^E F^{AB}\wedge F^{CD}.
\]

When \(V^2\) is fixed, its torsionless broken phase contains the MacDowell-Mansouri action and hence Einstein-Cartan gravity. When \(V^2\) varies, the coefficient of the four-dimensional Euler density varies. Expanding around a broken vacuum therefore produces

\[
f_E(\varphi)\mathcal G
=f_E(0)\mathcal G
+f_E'(0)\varphi\mathcal G+\cdots.
\]

The first term is topological and does not affect local bulk equations. The linear term is precisely the scalar-Gauss-Bonnet interaction, with

\[
\alpha=f_E'(0)
\]

up to the canonical normalization of \(\varphi\).

The cubic Weyl term is the leading parity-even, pure-gravity on-shell correction generated after integrating out short relational loops and heavy microscopic modes. Its coefficient must be computed by matching graph curvature cumulants to continuum amplitudes.

A complete derivation still requires the coupled Hessian of the radial Cartan mode and contorsion. Some general Cartan actions make contorsion propagate. The metric action above is valid only if those torsion modes are healthy and either retained explicitly or consistently integrated out below their mass.

## 3. Exact GR embedding

Set

\[
\alpha=0,
\qquad
c_e\ell_*^4=0,
\qquad
\varphi=\text{constant}.
\]

Then

\[
S_{\mathrm{RLU}+}\longrightarrow
\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int d^4x\sqrt{-g}R+S_m,
\]

which is GR exactly.

At finite coefficients, GR is recovered continuously whenever

\[
\left|\frac{\alpha}{L_{\mathcal R}^2}\right|\ll1,
\qquad
\left|c_e\right|
\left(\frac{\ell_*}{L_{\mathcal R}}\right)^4\ll1,
\]

where \(L_{\mathcal R}\) is the local curvature radius.

Thus RLU+ does not replace successful weak-field GR. It contains it as its low-curvature phase.

## 4. Linear-spectrum theorem

Expand around Minkowski space and a constant scalar background:

\[
g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu},
\qquad
\varphi=0+\delta\varphi.
\]

Since one curvature tensor begins at first order in \(h\),

\[
\mathcal G=O(h^2),
\qquad
\mathcal O_e=O(h^3).
\]

Therefore

\[
\varphi\mathcal G=O(\delta\varphi\,h^2)
\]

and neither beyond-GR operator contributes to the quadratic action. The complete quadratic action is

\[
\boxed{
S^{(2)}
=S_{\mathrm{Fierz-Pauli}}[h]
-\frac{1}{32\pi G}
\int d^4x\,\partial_\mu\delta\varphi\,
\partial^\mu\delta\varphi.
}
\]

Consequently the flat-background spectrum contains

\[
2\ \text{massless graviton helicities}
+1\ \text{healthy scalar mode}.
\]

There is no additional linear ghost pole. If the radial mode is heavy and removed from the low-energy spectrum, the pure cubic-Weyl branch retains only the two graviton helicities.

The cubic theory must nevertheless be treated perturbatively. Resumming a finite higher-derivative truncation as a fundamental action can create spurious modes not present in the EFT. Order reduction or an equivalent action-level elimination is part of the definition of the low-energy theory.

## 5. Radiative-stability improvement

Under a constant shift,

\[
\varphi\rightarrow\varphi+c,
\]

we have

\[
\Delta S
=\frac{\alpha c}{16\pi G}
\int d^4x\sqrt{-g}\,\mathcal G.
\]

For fixed topology and appropriate boundary treatment, this is a topological number. The local field equations are therefore shift invariant.

This is a major improvement over the earlier quadratically coupled symmetron-like proposal. A regulator preserving the shift symmetry cannot generate an ordinary local potential or a large scalar mass perturbatively. The previous order-\(10^{27}\) mass-squared naturalness problem is not inherited by this branch.

This statement is perturbative and local. Nonperturbative quantum-gravity effects or topology-changing configurations could still break the shift symmetry and must be bounded separately.

## 6. Exact black-hole scalar profile

Use the decoupling expansion in which the metric is Schwarzschild at zeroth order:

\[
ds^2
=-f(r)dt^2+\frac{dr^2}{f(r)}+r^2d\Omega^2,
\qquad
f(r)=1-\frac{2M}{r}.
\]

For Schwarzschild,

\[
\mathcal G=\frac{48M^2}{r^6}.
\]

The scalar equation is

\[
\Box\varphi=-\alpha\mathcal G,
\]

or

\[
\frac1{r^2}\frac{d}{dr}
\left(r^2f\frac{d\varphi}{dr}\right)
=-\frac{48\alpha M^2}{r^6}.
\]

The unique solution that is regular at the horizon and vanishes at infinity is

\[
\boxed{
\varphi(r)
=\frac{2\alpha}{M^2}
\left[
\frac Mr+\frac{M^2}{r^2}
+\frac{4M^3}{3r^3}
\right].
}
\]

Direct substitution gives exactly

\[
\Box\varphi+\alpha\mathcal G=0.
\]

At the horizon,

\[
\varphi(2M)=\frac{11\alpha}{6M^2},
\]

which is finite. At large radius,

\[
\varphi(r)=\frac{D}{r}+O(r^{-2}),
\]

with scalar charge

\[
\boxed{D=\frac{2\alpha}{M}.}
\]

The charge is secondary: it is fixed by the black-hole mass and the topological coupling rather than being an independent parameter. More generally, the scalar charge is controlled by the horizon surface gravity and Euler characteristic.

The scalar profile is \(O(\alpha)\). Its stress tensor and the metric correction begin at

\[
O(\alpha^2).
\]

This explains how RLU+ can produce a leading scalar-radiation signature while keeping the tensor metric close to GR.

## 7. Horizon-versus-star theorem

In the decoupling limit of the linear Gauss-Bonnet coupling:

\[
D_{\mathrm{regular\ star}}=0,
\qquad
D_{\mathrm{black\ hole}}=\frac{2\alpha}{M}
\]

in the normalization used here.

This creates a sharp topological distinction:

- ordinary horizonless stars do not carry the asymptotic monopole charge;
- black holes do;
- unequal black-hole binaries and black-hole–neutron-star binaries can radiate a scalar dipole;
- equal-mass black-hole binaries suppress that dipole.

Because

\[
\frac{D_i}{M_i}=\frac{2\alpha}{M_i^2},
\]

the dipole source is proportional to

\[
\Delta_{12}
=2\alpha
\left(\frac1{M_1^2}-\frac1{M_2^2}\right).
\]

It vanishes at equal mass and increases rapidly with mass asymmetry. The resulting inspiral correction enters at negative first post-Newtonian order in the standard scalar-radiation expansion.

## 8. Pure-gravity prediction: nonzero black-hole tidal response

The cubic-Weyl branch is

\[
S_{C^3}
=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int d^4x\sqrt{-g}
\left[R+c_e\ell_*^4\mathcal O_e\right].
\]

Because \(\mathcal O_e=O(h^3)\), it does not alter the flat-space propagator. Its strong-field expansion parameter for a Schwarzschild black hole is

\[
\epsilon_e
=c_e\left(\frac{\ell_*}{r_s}\right)^4,
\qquad
r_s=2M.
\]

Four-dimensional Schwarzschild black holes in GR have vanishing static bosonic Love numbers. Cubic curvature corrections generically create a nonzero tidal response. A direct fixed-\(\ell=2\) metric calculation gives the benchmark

\[
\Delta(B/A)_{\ell=2}=-2400\epsilon_e.
\]

This coefficient belongs to the stated fixed-multipole metric normalization and is not by itself the canonical analytically continued gauge-invariant Love number. Its value is nevertheless a reproducible strong-field benchmark.

For circular binaries, recent higher-derivative calculations find that the associated waveform and flux corrections first appear at fifth post-Newtonian order and are proportional to the quadrupolar Love response.

This branch is especially attractive because it can surpass GR without adding a light propagating field.

## 9. A cross-channel RLU consistency relation

Suppose one microscopic relational correlation length \(a\) generates both operators:

\[
\alpha=\zeta_E a^2,
\qquad
c_e\ell_*^4=\zeta_6a^4.
\]

Define the dimensionless microscopic ratio

\[
\boxed{
\rho_{\mathrm{RLU}}
=\frac{c_e\ell_*^4}{\alpha^2}
=\frac{\zeta_6}{\zeta_E^2}.
}
\]

The black-hole scalar charge gives

\[
\left(\frac DM\right)^2
=\frac{4\alpha^2}{M^4},
\]

while the cubic expansion parameter is

\[
\epsilon_e
=\frac{c_e\ell_*^4}{16M^4}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\epsilon_e}{(D/M)^2}
=\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{RLU}}}{64},
}
\]

which is independent of black-hole mass.

Using the fixed-quadrupole metric benchmark,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Delta(B/A)_{\ell=2}}{(D/M)^2}
=-\frac{75}{2}\rho_{\mathrm{RLU}}.
}
\]

This is the first genuinely unification-like beyond-GR target for RLU:

> the scalar dipole sector and the pure tensor tidal sector are not independent; their ratio is fixed by one dimensionless number produced by relational coarse-graining.

GR predicts both the new scalar charge and the cubic correction to vanish. A generic phenomenological modified-gravity fit treats the two coefficients independently. RLU would surpass both only if it computes \(\rho_{\mathrm{RLU}}\) from the microscopic graph and observations satisfy the mass-independent relation.

## 11. What would count as surpassing GR

RLU+ must satisfy all five conditions:

### Exact inheritance

It must reproduce all tested GR predictions in their measured regimes, including weak-field equivalence-principle tests, binary-pulsar timing, gravitational-wave propagation, and Kerr ringdown within existing errors.

### Internal health

The complete gauge-fixed Hessian in the variables

\[
(h_{\mu\nu},\varphi,C^{IJ}{}_{\mu})
\]

must have positive residues for physical modes, no gradient instability, and a hyperbolic initial-value problem throughout the claimed EFT domain.

### Microscopic prediction

The relational action must calculate, rather than fit independently,

\[
\alpha,
\qquad
c_e\ell_*^4,
\qquad
\rho_{\mathrm{RLU}},
\]

including signs and RG running.

### Distinct observational pattern

The same coefficients must fit:

- black-hole scalar charge and dipole radiation;
- fifth-post-Newtonian tidal effects;
- black-hole ringdown shifts;
- the absence of stellar scalar monopole charge;
- mass scaling across multiple systems.

### Statistical superiority

Data must prefer the nonzero RLU coefficient set over GR after penalizing the extra parameters. Merely being able to reproduce GR is not enough.

## 12. Immediate microscopic computation

The Stage-4 graph action must now be matched not only to TEGR but to the first irrelevant operators. For each blocked action, project the one-particle-irreducible vertices onto:

\[
\Gamma^{(2)}\rightarrow
\text{Fierz-Pauli normalization and scalar/torsion Hessian},
\]

\[
\Gamma^{(3)}_{hhh}\rightarrow c_e\ell_*^4,
\]

\[
\Gamma_{\varphi hh}^{(3)}\rightarrow\alpha,
\]

and calculate

\[
\rho_{\mathrm{RLU}}
=\frac{c_e\ell_*^4}{\alpha^2}.
\]

The matching conditions are:

1. the two-derivative sector flows to the TEGR/Einstein ray;
2. the scalar kinetic residue is positive;
3. torsion poles are absent below the cutoff or have positive residues;
4. the parity-odd projections vanish if microscopic orientation reversal is exact;
5. \(\rho_{\mathrm{RLU}}\) approaches the same nonzero number under at least two blocking rules;
6. the result survives increasing graph size and decreasing lattice spacing.

A universal nonzero \(\rho_{\mathrm{RLU}}\) would be RLU's first concrete prediction beyond GR. A flow to \(\rho_{\mathrm{RLU}}=0\) would mean the theory recovers GR but does not surpass it through this channel.
